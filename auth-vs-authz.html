Authentication vs Authorisation

Two words a letter apart, and the single most common mix-up in web security. One proves who you are. The other decides what you're allowed to do. Almost every access-control bug is an app that gets the first right and the second wrong.

The two questions

AuthN

Authentication

"Who are you?"
Proves
Your identity.
Happens
First — at the door, when you log in.
Mechanisms
Password, MFA, session cookie, token.
When it fails
Anyone can impersonate you. → Broken Authentication.
AuthZ

Authorisation

"Are you allowed to do this?"
Proves
Your permissions.
Happens
After — on every sensitive action.
Mechanisms
Roles, ownership checks, access control rules.
When it fails
You reach data or actions that aren't yours. → Access Control, Privilege Escalation.
Order matters: authentication is the first gate, authorisation the second. Passing the first never means you pass the second — being logged in is not the same as being allowed.
